## 1. Hook (0:00~0:15)

The college majors with the lowest unemployment rate right now aren't in tech. They're in teaching. Special education grads: zero point seven percent. [#2]

`[COLLAGE] a torn ranking strip: "SPECIAL EDUCATION 0.7%" on top, "COMPUTER SCIENCE 7.0%" far below`

So which majors are actually safe from AI?

`[TITLE] WHICH MAJORS ARE AI-PROOF?`

## 2. The honest answer first (0:15~0:40)

No major is AI-proof. Nobody's data can promise that. What the New York Fed data can show is narrower: in 2024, which majors' new grads were finding work, and what they were paid. [#1]

`[KINETIC] "AI-PROOF" crossed out → "WHO'S GETTING HIRED. WHAT IT PAYS."`

## 3. Safe by one measure (0:40~1:20)

Start with the lowest unemployment. Education majors dominate the top. Special education at zero point seven percent, elementary education at one point two. [#2]

`[CHART] ranked strips: Special ed 0.7 · Misc. education 1.1 · Elementary ed 1.2 · All majors 4.2 (dashed)`

But look at the pay. Special education grads start around forty-six thousand dollars. Elementary education, forty-five. The typical starting salary across all majors is fifty-eight thousand. [#2, #1]

`[CHART] same majors, pay: $46,000 · $45,000 · All majors $58,000`

Safe by one measure. Among the lowest paid by another.

## 4. The sweet spot (1:20~2:20)

The more useful list is the majors that do well on both. Below-average unemployment, and starting pay of seventy thousand or more. [#3]

`[CHART] a scatter of torn dots: unemployment across, starting pay up; the low-left/high corner lights up`

Nursing: two point one percent unemployed, seventy thousand to start. And only about thirteen percent of nursing grads are underemployed, against thirty-nine percent overall. [#3, #4, #1]

Civil engineering: two point three percent, seventy-five thousand. Aerospace engineering: two point two percent, eighty-five thousand. Construction services: two point two percent, seventy-five thousand. [#3]

Electrical engineering, finance, and economics make the list too. [#3]

`[CHART] the sweet-spot cards pinned in a row: Nursing · Civil · Aerospace · Construction · Electrical · Finance · Economics`

Compare computer science: higher pay to start, at eighty-seven thousand, but seven percent unemployed. [#5]

## 5. One pattern, not proof (2:20~3:10)

Many of the sweet-spot majors lead to work tied to a place, a license, or a patient: hospitals, job sites, aircraft, power grids. That's our reading, not something the table measures. [#3]

`[COLLAGE] four small cards: a hospital bed, a bridge, a wing, a power line — drawn, not photographed`

It lines up with two findings from our first video. The New York Fed found the gap for young grads opened in jobs that can be done remotely, like software engineering. [#6]

And Stanford payroll data shows employment in the jobs most exposed to AI fell about eleven percent from late 2022 to mid-2026, while in the least exposed jobs it rose about ten. [#7]

`[CHART] two torn bars: Most AI-exposed −11% · Least exposed +10%`

Patterns, not proof. And these figures are one year, 2024, with small samples for some majors.

## 6. Close (3:10~3:40)

So, which majors are AI-proof? None, honestly. But some majors are doing well on both counts that matter for a first job: getting hired, and getting paid. [#3]

A better question than "is it AI-proof?" is this: does this major lead to work that AI helps with, or work that AI does? And what does it pay?
